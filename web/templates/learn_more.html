01 / The process

From a weather forecast to a useful next step.

1

Detect the cold

Weather forecasts identify upcoming freezing stretches that could put heating systems under pressure.

2

Put history in context

The model uses past complaints, violations and building information to estimate risk and prioritize properties.

3

Help people prepare

Tenants can check a building, landlords can review a property’s record, and inspectors can explore the ranked queue.

Built by NYU Tingineers for the IBM Z Datathon, with model work on IBM LinuxONE. HeatAhead is an independent project, not NYC311 or an HPD service.

02 / Read the map

Color and height tell different stories.

Color = modeled winter risk

Lower riskHigher risk

A hexagon shows the average risk of its displayed properties. Zoomed-in property markers show individual estimates.

Height = past complaints

In the citywide 3D view, taller hexagons mean more heating complaints last season. They are not a count of buildings or their actual height.

Click an area, then choose a property

A hexagon opens its building list, sorted by risk. Use its pages to see every member. A list entry or an individual marker opens that property’s record.

Zoom in for building context

Hexagons gradually fade into property markers. Gray footprints and heights come from the map. A tax lot can contain multiple physical buildings.

Buildings without a visible hexagon or marker

The background map includes buildings beyond our ranked data. Missing matched history or coordinates can leave a property without a mapped estimate, and the minimum-risk filter can hide lower scores. An uncolored area does not prove there have been no complaints or that heat is working.

In 3D, a hexagon with zero recorded complaints last season has zero height. Switch to Flat map to see its risk color. At street level, look for colored property markers as the hexagons fade.

03 / Understand the estimate

A probability is a reason to look closer.

Match the time window

Winter risk describes a season. The cold-day estimate on a building record describes a shorter prediction window. A seasonal estimate and a short-window estimate answer different questions.

Check the evidence

On a building record, expand How accurate is this number? to see predictions grouped against observed outcomes in held-out data. Results for a group do not guarantee an outcome for one building.

  • High risk is not a confirmed outage or inspected violation. Low risk does not guarantee working heat.
  • Complaint records reflect reporting behavior, and unreported problems can be missed.
  • Probabilities are capped at 99%. Above 20%, the site shows bands to avoid unsupported precision.
  • A property that is not individually ranked has no individual estimate; that does not mean zero risk.

Already without heat or hot water?

Contact your landlord or managing agent. If the problem is not resolved, use NYC311 to report it. Give service and contact details directly to the City. You do not need a high model score to report a real problem.

05 / Data & privacy

Public records, with practical limits.

HeatAhead combines public NYC complaint, violation, property, owner and boiler records with weather information. Records can be incomplete, delayed or hard to match. A complaint is a report; a violation follows a different inspection process.

HPD complaints ↗ HPD violations ↗ NYC PLUTO properties ↗ Open-Meteo weather ↗

The map shows building-level information. Account registrations link saved buildings to a verified Google email. HeatAhead does not receive your Google password. Public owner and agent details come from business-registration records.

The site guide uses the answers published in our FAQs. Common questions are matched on your device. If optional AI matching is enabled, an unfamiliar question may be sent to the AI provider to select a help topic. The guide does not send your account identity or conversation history, and this application does not store guide conversations. Do not enter personal contact details, passwords or unit details.

Aerial photos and Street View have varying capture dates; they are not live images. The guide can point you to a page, but cannot send a notice, file a complaint, change alerts or invent a building score.
