Start with one building

What to check first

  1. Find the right address

    Open Check a building. Enter its street address and borough, or its 10-digit BBL. A BBL identifies a property in NYC's records. Leave out your apartment number.

  2. Read the score alongside its history

    Check the winter-risk estimate, then look at past heating complaints and violations. Complaints are reports of a problem; they do not by themselves confirm an inspection finding.

  3. Choose your next step

    Review the available owner or agent details, draft a message, or sign in to save the building for alerts. You choose whether to send a drafted message.

One property record can cover more than one physical building. A missing score means we don't have an estimate for that property.

Read the map

Color and height tell you different things

Color shows estimated winter risk

Lower riskHigher risk

Darker blue means higher estimated risk. A hexagon shows the average score of the properties inside it. Up close, each colored dot shows one property's score.

Height shows recorded complaints

Taller hexagons contain more heating complaints recorded last season. Height does not show how many buildings are in the area, or how tall those buildings are.

Switch to Flat map if you want to compare colors without the raised columns.

Click an area
A hexagon opens a list of its properties, with higher scores first. Use Next for more results or Zoom to these buildings for a closer look. Your risk filter still applies.
Open one property
Zoom in for individual dots, or choose a building from the area list. Its record shows the available risk, history and owner details.
Find a blank area
Gray shapes belong to the background map. A property may have missing records or coordinates, or your filter may hide its score. In 3D, a hexagon with no recorded complaints last season stays flat. Try Flat map, reset the filter, or look up the address.

A building without color can still have heating problems.

Understand the scores

Check the time period before comparing numbers

Winter risk

Estimates the chance of a heating problem during the winter. This is the score used to color the map.

Cold-day risk

Estimates the chance of a heating problem over a 48-hour window around cold weather. It answers a shorter-term question, so it can differ from winter risk.

These estimates come from patterns in public complaint, building and weather records. HeatAhead does not measure the temperature inside a building or confirm whether its heating is working right now.

  • A higher score suggests a closer look. It does not mean the heat has already failed. A low score does not rule out a problem.
  • Some reports are missing. Problems that were never reported may not appear in the building's history.
  • Scores have limits. From 20% up, the site shows ranges; no displayed probability goes above 99%.

On a building's page, open How accurate is this number? to compare groups of predictions with recorded outcomes. For the training steps and evaluation results, read About & methods.

Data & privacy

What sits behind the page

HeatAhead combines public NYC building, complaint, violation, owner and boiler records with weather data. Records can be incomplete or out of date. Photos show when they were taken; they are not live views.

The public map uses building-level records. Saving a building links it to your signed-in account for alerts. The help chat answers common questions on your device; if optional AI matching is enabled, it can send an unfamiliar question to a provider to suggest a published help topic.
